# Math Sprout Exercise Milestones

Suggested GitHub milestones for expanding Math Sprout beyond the four basic operations. The sequence starts with the brief's three suggested first steps, then broadens into visual exercises, number sense, puzzles, and game mechanics.

## 1. First New Exercise Types

Add three small, high-value exercise formats on top of the existing arithmetic practice: **Missing Number** questions that place the blank in different positions, **Share the Cookies** drag-and-drop division, and **Number Pyramid** puzzles. Keep the interactions touch-first, offer encouraging explanations after mistakes, and support both phone and tablet layouts.

## 2. Visual Number Sense

Build exercises that make quantity and arithmetic visible: **Number Line Jump**, **Bags of Marbles**, and **Arrays / Dot Grids**. Use these visual models to help children understand addition, multiplication, and the relationship between factors instead of only recalling answers.

## 3. Mental Math and Estimation

Add short exercises for **Make 10 / Make 100**, **Compare**, **Round & Estimate**, and **Times Table Patterns**. Let children answer with large tap targets and scale the number range and complexity gradually.

## 4. Everyday Math and Fractions

Introduce practical contexts through **Coins & Money** with euros and cents, **Clock & Time**, **Measuring**, and **Fractions with Pizza**. Use interactive objects such as draggable coins, rotatable clock hands, measuring tools, and tappable or draggable pizza slices.

## 5. Logic and Math Puzzles

Add **Number Patterns**, **Mini Magic Square**, **Math Maze**, **Word Problems**, and **Guess My Number**. Keep instructions clear for young readers, make names and themes customizable where relevant, and provide hints or visual explanations when an answer is missed.

## 6. Adaptive and Accessible Practice

Make difficulty respond to a child's progress: increase it after a few correct answers and revisit easier examples after repeated mistakes. Ensure feedback teaches how to solve the problem, allow children to work at their own pace, and provide clear visuals, readable typography, and strong contrast. Any timer must be optional and must not penalize a child for taking more time.

## 7. Practice Round Variations

Add optional ways to replay and vary practice: a **Relaxed Timer** focused on a child's own record, a mixed-operation **Boss Round**, and a **Daily Challenge** with a streak that has no penalty for missed days. Keep short sessions and preserve a no-pressure way to practise.

## 8. Grow Your Garden

Reward completed practice rounds with visible garden progress, such as a plant growing or a new part of the garden appearing. Make rewards encouraging and inclusive rather than dependent on speed, perfect scores, or an unbroken streak.

## 9. Two-Player Practice

Explore a same-device **Two-Player Duel** designed for tablets, with a clear, touch-friendly area for each child. Keep this optional and ensure the core single-player experience remains usable on phones.

## Shared acceptance criteria

Apply these criteria to every milestone:

- Use touch targets of at least 48×48 px, preferably larger.
- Prefer tapping, dragging, and custom answer controls over the system keyboard.
- Support portrait and landscape layouts on phones and tablets.
- Give instant, friendly feedback and show a useful way to solve missed questions.
- Keep practice rounds short and timers optional.
